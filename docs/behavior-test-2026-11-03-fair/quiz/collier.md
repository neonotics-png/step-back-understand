# Jacob Collier: Comprehension Quiz

1. In his arrangement of "In the Bleak Midwinter," Collier modulates from E major to a key roughly a quarter tone above G (often called "G half-sharp"). Why can't this be done on a normally tuned piano, and how does the chord-to-chord mechanism let singers reach that key smoothly and still stay in tune at every step?

2. What is "negative harmony" (how do you get a chord's "negative" counterpart in a key), where did the idea come from before Collier, and how does Collier actually use it in his own music?

3. Collier talks about chords and modes as "brighter" or "darker" and describes Lydian as the brightest mode (his "Super-Ultra-Hyper-Mega-Meta Lydian"). What is the circle-of-fifths reasoning behind that brightness scale, and what does it let him do when he arranges?

4. Collier says that "every single melody note works with every single bass note." What does that mean in practice for reharmonization, and why is it a different way of thinking from "find the chord that contains the melody note"?

5. Collier's signature vocal stacks are dozens or hundreds of overdubs of his own voice. Describe how he builds them (how the parts relate to each other and where that habit comes from), and explain why he tunes and times them by ear and by hand instead of pitch-correcting to the grid.

6. Collier's studio arrangements are one-man multitracks. Explain the two main ways he recreates dense, many-part harmony live, the vocal Harmonizer and the audience choir, and what each requires of him as a performer in real time.

7. The four-volume *Djesse* project changes sound a lot from volume to volume. What was its organizing principle, if not genre, and what did Collier see as the main arranging and production challenge of a project built that way?

8. What is the most serious recurring critique of Collier's music from people who respect his skill? Use a concrete example, such as Ethan Hein's comparison of Collier's reharmonization of "Don't You Worry 'Bout a Thing" with Stevie Wonder's original, and say what the critique shows about the trade-offs in his approach.
