# ADÉLA, PRIMA: quiz answers (from memory, no lookups)

**1. "KGB"**
She plays a spy, an outside observer who studies and absorbs American pop culture. The song paraphrases how she taught herself English as a child from Disney Channel and American TV. It casts her as a foreigner from the former Eastern Bloc who trained relentlessly to infiltrate American pop, with the Soviet secret police as a metaphor for that discipline and surveillance-like study. The video carries an onscreen disclaimer: the imagery acknowledges where she is from, it is not an endorsement, and she stands with the people of Ukraine.

**2. "Ain't In LA"**
It draws on her mother's youth in communist Czechoslovakia, where Western media was banned. Her grandfather smuggled American pop posters home from his mining work in West Germany, and her mother dreamed of LA. The video was shot in Bratislava but filled with Hollywood and LA imagery. Its message, in her words, is "You don't have to leave your country," for people who "weren't born in the right place, at the right time." So the album, which opens with ambition aimed at breaking into America, ends by saying the dream doesn't require moving to LA. Musically it nods to 2013-era pop, and Theron Thomas, co-writer of Miley Cyrus's "We Can't Stop," raps the refrain.

**3. "The hard with the soft"**
She meant pairing vulnerability with swagger, and muscular, bass-heavy beats with feminine energy, so the album would "sound exactly like a 22-year-old girl." The upbeat side is a song like "Boys," a club stomper that critics compared to Britney and to Missy Elliott and the Neptunes. "Therapy" is the soft side: a quiet ballad over sparse piano and heavy Auto-Tune about missing her sister, who lives far away. She recorded it while sick and nearly voiceless, so she half-talks it. Reports differ on whether the sister is older or younger.

**4. Why PRIMA, and why polished pop**
"Prima" means "first" because this is her debut. It is also prima ballerina, the top rank in a ballet company, a nod to her ballet training from age three. She has said "I'm trying to be the prima ballerina of pop, and this is my first step." The mainstream aim fits her goals. She has said the only music she ever listened to was pop, her formative albums were *Bangerz*, *My Everything* and Beyoncé's self-titled, and at 15 she wrote goals like getting to LA and getting signed. Hitmaker Blake Slatkin co-executive produced alongside Dylan Brady. I don't recall her directly explaining the move away from the EP's hyperpop. That reasoning is my inference, and reviewers noted the shift.

**5. Frankness about sex and the body**
She describes herself as very blunt, with music that is "cheeky" and "uncomfortably honest," and ties that to being an Eastern European woman and to her mother, "the most blunt person I know." Artistically, her persona has been built on provocation since the EP: her debut EP was titled *The Provocateur*, and its cover image was deliberately confrontational. She has also said her ethos embraces imperfection, and that she does "exactly what I wanted to" with full creative control. I didn't research the specific sexual content of "Hitachi" or "I'm the Man" in detail. I know "Hitachi" as her first full ballad and "I'm the Man" as a gender-role swagger track, so I can't speak to their lyrics beyond that.

**6. The path to PRIMA**
In 2023 she competed on HYBE and Geffen's *The Debut: Dream Academy*, the show that formed KATSEYE, and was cut in the first elimination round. Netflix's *Pop Star Academy: KATSEYE* then gave her a "villain" edit, and she has called that year the worst of her life. A line from a HYBE x Geffen executive, "We see Adéla as a solo artist," became a fan tagline. She stayed in LA, released "Homewrecked" in September 2024 (a revenge fantasy about her father's mistress), followed by "Superscar," the Grimes-co-produced "Machine Girl" and others. These led to the EP *The Provocateur* (August 2025, Capitol), a conceptual, provocative, more hyperpop and industrial project. That path gave her an image as a provocative outsider. *PRIMA* keeps the attitude but is glossier and more mainstream, with Slatkin and Brady, who had worked on a few EP tracks, now running the whole album.

**7. Critical reception**
Reception was generally favorable but split, with a Metacritic score around 69. Clash (8/10) and the Sydney Morning Herald (5/5) were enthusiastic, Rolling Stone and NME gave 3.5/5, and Pitchfork (5.7) and The Line of Best Fit (6/10) were cooler. The main disagreement was whether its confident, maximal pop was star quality or derivative. Skeptics said it wears its influences too heavily, "tries too hard" (as on "KGB"), and has moments that sound like others ("Starving Artist" like Tate McRae). Admirers praised its ambition and coherence. Both sides tended to agree that "Ain't In LA" and "Nicole Kidman" were standouts, and "Hitachi" was also widely praised.

**8. The expected revenge record**
They'd find a record about ambition, identity, her Slovak roots and her family, America-as-dream, Hollywood fantasy ("Nicole Kidman"), swagger and vulnerability. It is not an album aimed at KATSEYE. That fits how she has handled the history. She has said she didn't truly think she was right for the project, that she loves "the girls" and that "everything happens for a reason," and she stayed close with KATSEYE's Lara Raj. Raj even appears in uncredited spoken bits on "Boys." The swagger reads more as proving herself than as bitterness. Her one overt revenge song, "Homewrecked," was about her father's mistress, not the group.
