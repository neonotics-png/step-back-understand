# ADÉLA: *PRIMA* (debut album, 2026)

## Quick facts
- **Artist:** ADÉLA is Adéla Jergová, a Slovak singer, songwriter and dancer born in Bratislava in 2003.
- **Release date:** 4 September 2026.
- **Label:** Capitol Records. The FADER and Universal Music Canada both say Capitol. The Line of Best Fit names Polydor, which is probably the UK release, since both labels belong to Universal.
- **Length:** 11 tracks, about 31 minutes.
- **Executive producers:** Dylan Brady (of 100 gecs) and Blake Slatkin. Cashmere Cat produced "I'm The Man."
- **Singles:** "KGB," "Red Bottoms," "Ain't In LA" (released with the album announcement on 23 July 2026) and "Nicole Kidman."

## Who she is (context for the album)
- She trained in ballet through her teens, including time at the Vienna State Ballet and the English National Ballet School. She switched to pop during the COVID-19 pandemic.
- In 2023 she competed on *Dream Academy*, the HYBE x Geffen show that formed the girl group KATSEYE. She was eliminated early, finishing joint last in a televised cut. Netflix's 2024 docuseries *Pop Star Academy: KATSEYE* covered this, and that is where many fans first saw her.
- She released the singles "Homewrecked" and "Superscar" in 2024 and the Grimes co-produced "Machine Girl" in February 2025. Capitol signed her in May 2025.
- Her debut EP, *The Provocateur*, came out in August 2025. It included "SexOnTheBeat," which has a cameo from Christina Aguilera. The EP was shortlisted for a Czech Anděl Award.
- In 2026 she opened for Demi Lovato's North American tour before her own headline run, the Red Bottoms Tour.

## The title and concept
- "PRIMA means 'first', which is perfect since this is my debut." The title is also a nod to the *prima ballerina*, the lead dancer of a company. She says: "I'm trying to be the prima ballerina of pop, and this is my first step to achieve that."
- Her stated aim: "I want this album to sound exactly like a 22-year-old girl." She describes mixing "the hard with the soft": vulnerable moments next to swagger, and muscular beats next to feminine energy.
- **Lyrical themes:** insecurity, toxic relationships, identity and autonomy, ambition and childhood dreams of pop stardom, romance, self-confidence and family.

## Sound
- Glossy, club-leaning electronic pop with dance production and touches of hyperpop. The hyperpop comes from Brady, and the polished pop craft comes from Slatkin.
- Clash compares "Boys" to Missy Elliott and the Neptunes' turn-of-the-millennium work.
- "Ain't In LA" leans toward mid-2010s pop and hip-hop and is built around a pitched-down vocal sample.
- Several critics note that the record is more mid-tempo and ballad-heavy, and more mainstream, than the abrasive electropop of *The Provocateur*.

## The key song: "Ain't In LA"
- She wrote it after a trip home to Bratislava. A photo of her mother at 18, posing in front of a wall of Western pop-culture images, prompted it. In her words: "I wrote this for her and the billion other bitches who are probably cooler than me."
- Fred Gervais directed the video. It was shot in Bratislava and is packed with LA and Hollywood references.
- It was her first Billboard Hot 100 entry, debuting at No. 85 and climbing to No. 61. It also charted in the UK.

## Commercial performance
- The album debuted at **No. 4 on the Billboard 200** with about **56,000 equivalent album units**, made up of about 31,000 pure sales plus about 26 million on-demand streams. Forecasts had been around 40,000–45,000 units.
- It reached No. 1 on Spotify's Top Albums Debut chart, both in the US and globally.
- According to Wikipedia, it went to No. 1 in the Netherlands and Belgium and reached the top 10 in several other countries, including the UK and US. I did not check the country positions independently.

## Critical reception: mixed to positive
- **Clash, 8/10:** an ambitious, coherent pop record with deliberate sequencing, such as "Fantasize" into "Starving Artist." The review highlights "KGB," "Nicole Kidman," "I'm The Man," "Boys" and "Hitachi," and calls the last one of her strongest vocals.
- **The Daily Illini, 9/10:** a solid showcase of her artistry and personality.
- **The Line of Best Fit, 6/10:** "plays dress up with pop history." It finds the album weaker than *The Provocateur*, though it praises "Red Bottoms" as its best take on a traditional pop ballad.
- **Sputnik Music, 3.0/5:** the album is slowed down by too many mid-tempo songs and ballads, but "Ain't In LA" is a radio-ready anthem with a great singalong hook.
- **Overall:** critics see a charismatic star with a few standout songs on an uneven album, and a deliberate step from underground hyperpop toward the mainstream.
