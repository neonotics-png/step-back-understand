# ADÉLA, *PRIMA* (2026): what it is and how to understand it

*Research notes compiled 2026-11-03 from 38 web searches and page reads. Where sources disagree, this note says so.*

## Who ADÉLA is (the context the album relies on)

- **Ballet first.** She started ballet at three, danced the lead in *The Nutcracker* at the Slovak National Theatre as a child, and trained at the Vienna State Ballet and then the English National Ballet School in London. COVID sent her home, and she switched to music. She performs in pointe shoes, and the ballet background is central to her persona and to the album title. (Sources: Wikipedia, ynetnews, W Magazine.)
- **The KATSEYE "reject."** In 2023 she was one of 20 finalists on *The Debut: Dream Academy*, the HYBE x Geffen competition that formed the girl group KATSEYE. She was cut in the first elimination round. Netflix's 2024 docuseries *Pop Star Academy: KATSEYE* gave her a "villain edit." An executive's line, "We see Adéla as a solo artist," became a fan tagline. (Sources: W Magazine, ynetnews, Wikipedia.)
- **Going solo.** Her releases were "Homewrecked" (Sept 2024), "Superscar," and "Machine Girl" (2025, co-produced by Grimes). She signed with Capitol in 2025 and released *The Provocateur* EP on 22 Aug 2025. That EP was abrasive "industrial pop" built around a provocative, rage-bait persona, and critics liked it.
- **She taught herself English** by watching Disney Channel and pop-star interviews. She calls herself a "student of pop." Albums she says shaped her include Ariana Grande's *My Everything*, Miley Cyrus's *Bangerz*, and Beyoncé's self-titled album.

## The concept

- **Title.** "PRIMA means 'first', which is perfect since this is my debut." It also refers to the *prima ballerina*, the top-ranked soloist in a ballet company: "I'm trying to be the prima ballerina of pop, and this is my first step to achieve that."
- **Thesis.** "I want this album to sound exactly like a 22-year-old girl." She describes the mix as hard and soft, vulnerability and swagger, "pounding beats and feminine energy."
- **Themes** (from the label and critics): ambition and pop-star mythmaking, an outsider from Eastern Europe absorbing American pop culture, insecurity and perfectionism, toxic relationships, sex-positive desire, reversed gender roles, guilt about family left behind, and pride in where you come from.
- **Visual world.** Creative direction and styling came from Chris Horan, a longtime Charli xcx collaborator. He drew on "Slavic Doll realness" from mid-2000s runways and on 1990s Eastern European rave fashion. Her choreography is deliberately campy: "wink-wink energy, almost ironic." She has cotton-candy pink hair.

## Track by track

The writer and producer details come from Wikipedia, Songfacts and press releases. ADÉLA's own comments come mainly from her Capital FM "Making The Album" interview.

1. **KGB** (2:38). Lead single (17 Apr 2026), produced by Slatkin, Brady and The Dare. Its central metaphor casts her as a spy infiltrating the American pop industry: the girl from the former Eastern Bloc who drilled herself relentlessly to get in. The dance-pop and electropop beat has tech-house touches, and its dance break went viral. The video, shot in the LA Theatre and directed by Rachel Dunkel, draws on her ballet training. W Magazine reports that the video ends with a disclaimer that the Soviet imagery is not an endorsement and that she stands with Ukraine. I found only that one source for the disclaimer. Critics split on the song: Ratings Game rated it 5/5, Line of Best Fit called it a strong opener, and Rolling Stone said it "tries too hard."
2. **Nicole Kidman** (3:01). Became the album's biggest hit. It was written by Adéla, Julia Michaels, Billy Walsh and Uffie, and produced by Slatkin and Brady. It samples God's Gospel Angels' "In My Father's House" (1986), which gives it a sped-up-soul feel. The idea came from a Golden Globes afterparty in January 2026, where her corset was so tight she "felt like" Kidman, and from her eventual love of *Eyes Wide Shut*. The chorus references *Moulin Rouge!* and *Babygirl*. The video, directed by Hannah Lux Davis, recreates scenes from Kidman's films and nods to her AMC cinema ad. Kidman herself said at the *Practical Magic 2* London premiere: "I absolutely know who she is... I'm going to go see her." She then debuted on TikTok using an ADÉLA song.
3. **I'm the Man** (2:30). Produced by Cashmere Cat. It flips gender roles: "I need men that are comfortable with themselves enough to be down to be the baby girl." Some critics said its concept is a pop commonplace.
4. **Boys** (2:24). A club track set in the club bathroom about men who hover without engaging. Critics compare it to Britney Spears and The Neptunes, and to Missy Elliott. It includes uncredited spoken interludes from KATSEYE's Lara Raj, a friendly nod back to *Dream Academy*.
5. **Red Bottoms** (3:06). Second single (12 June 2026), with a video directed by Emma Drew Berson. Her "first heartbreak ballad" uses worn-out Louboutin red soles as a metaphor for a draining relationship full of mixed signals and fear of abandonment. On perfectionism she says, "I almost feel like I play a version of myself." It also gave its name to her headline tour.
6. **Hitachi** (3:36). Named after the Hitachi Magic Wand vibrator. It started as an upbeat song and became "a realer song about troubles in your relationship," about intimacy fading. Critics split sharply: Clash and The Post called it a standout vocal and an excellent ballad, while Line of Best Fit called it a "half-baked piano ballad."
7. **Fantasize** (3:03). About desire outside a passionless relationship ("life is too short"). It has a breathy vocal and a much-discussed 90s-style trombone that some critics loved and others found flat. The physical edition has an extended intro.
8. **Starving Artist** (3:00). About going back to a broke ex who has worked hard ("No shame in that"). It has a slowed-down, bass-heavy outro that critics likened to Gwen Stefani's "Hollaback Girl." One reviewer reads tracks 6 to 8 as a mini-trilogy of longing, revenge and stardom.
9. **Marijuana** (1:50). The shortest track, a 2010s-nostalgic, late-night interlude. Line of Best Fit says she styles herself as "the next Madonna" on it. She notes she doesn't smoke or use drugs: "Don't do drugs." Ringtone called it the best deep cut.
10. **Therapy** (3:00). Widely seen as the emotional core of the album. It is a quiet, distorted, partly hyperpop-processed ballad about older-sister guilt over being away from her younger sister while chasing a career abroad ("I wish I could have been there for her more"). She wrote it while ill on the road. Rolling Stone made it the centre of its review, while The Post found the whispery processed vocal hard to listen to.
11. **Ain't in LA** (3:04). Third single (23 July 2026), and the breakout. It is synth-pop and hip-hop with booming 808s, glitchy drums and chopped-and-screwed vocals. Theron Thomas, a co-writer and voice on Miley Cyrus's "We Can't Stop," delivers the distorted refrain. The song was inspired by a photo of her mother at 18 posing in front of Western pop posters that her grandfather had smuggled in from West Germany into communist Czechoslovakia. It is "for my mom and the billion other bitches who are probably cooler than me." Its message is that talent and interesting lives exist outside LA. The video was shot in her hometown, Bratislava, and directed by Fred Gervais.

**Bonus tracks:** "Nasty Dirty Gross" (website edition) and "Kiss Here" (iTunes edition). Physical editions use different second-verse lyrics on "Nicole Kidman."

**Key collaborators:** Blake Slatkin, Dylan Brady, The Dare, Cashmere Cat, Julia Michaels, Billy Walsh, Uffie, Ali Tamposi, Theron Thomas, and Count Baldor. Pino Palladino plays bass and Rob Moose arranged strings. Serban Ghenea and Manny Marroquin are among the mixers.

## Commercial performance

- **US.** The album debuted at **No. 4 on the Billboard 200** (chart dated 19 Sept 2026) with **56,000 equivalent units, including 31,000 pure sales**, beating 40–45K forecasts. It went **No. 1 on Top Album Sales** (and, per some reports, Vinyl Albums) and No. 1 on Spotify's US and global Top Albums Debut charts.
- **UK.** The album debuted at **No. 2** with 12,253 units, behind Kasabian. It is reported as the first album by a Slovak artist to enter the chart.
- **Elsewhere.** No. 1 in Slovakia, the Netherlands, Flanders and Scotland. Top 10 in Austria, Germany, Australia, Canada, the Czech Republic, Ireland, New Zealand, Poland and Nordic countries.
- **"Ain't in LA."** Her first Billboard Hot 100 entry and the **first song by a Slovak artist ever to chart on the Hot 100**. It debuted at No. 85. Reported peaks vary by date: No. 61 early, and later reports say **No. 24**. It peaked at No. 12 in the UK, No. 1 on the Billboard Philippines chart, and in the top 10 on Spotify Global.
- **"Nicole Kidman."** It entered the Hot 100 at No. 45 on album release and climbed into the **top 10**, the first Hot 100 top 10 by a Slovak artist. Most sources, including Wikipedia, say it peaked at **No. 6**. One outlet said No. 9, which likely reflects an earlier week. It reached **No. 4 in the UK** (week of 25 Sept 2026) and No. 3 on the Billboard Global 200. Its TikTok trend drew cameos from Madonna, Dua Lipa, Pedro Pascal and Kristen Wiig, among others.
- **Touring.** She played Lollapalooza on 2 Aug 2026. Her Red Bottoms Tour (London, 25 Aug 2026, to Warsaw, Feb 2027) sold out its North American and European legs. She made her *Tonight Show* debut on 22 Oct 2026 with a Moulin Rouge-styled medley of "Ain't in LA" and "Nicole Kidman."

I could not open the Guardian, Pitchfork, Variety, Billboard, NME, Slant and SMH reviews directly. Their scores and quotes come from Wikipedia's reception section and a Her Campus roundup.

**Recurring praise:**
- Star quality and wit.
- Tight 31-minute runtime and sequencing.
- The singles ("Ain't in LA," "Nicole Kidman") are undeniable.
- Real vulnerability on "Therapy" and "Red Bottoms."
- The family and immigrant story behind "Ain't in LA" and "KGB."

**Recurring criticism:**
- It trades the weird, abrasive industrial pop of *The Provocateur* for a safer, more mainstream, nostalgia-driven sound.
- It wears its influences (Britney and the Neptunes, Ariana Grande, Miley's *Bangerz* era, 2013–2016 pop) too openly.
- Some find the slow songs and mid-tempo stretch flat.
- Several critics compare it to Addison Rae's 2025 debut.

## How to make sense of it

1. **It is a pop-history costume album on purpose.** ADÉLA learned English and pop from American TV, and *PRIMA* is her act of studying and reproducing the pop she romanticized from Bratislava. Admirers hear a "student of pop" acing the exam. Detractors hear dress-up ("plays dress up with pop history," in Line of Best Fit's headline).
2. **The outsider story is the spine.** "KGB" (the spy from the East), "Therapy" (the sister she left behind) and "Ain't in LA" (her mother's smuggled posters) frame everything in between. The middle of the record is a run of relationship songs about desire, perfectionism and power ("I'm the Man," "Boys," "Red Bottoms," "Hitachi," "Fantasize," "Starving Artist").
3. **The KATSEYE rejection is the meta-narrative.** Cut in round one and edited as a villain, she ended up with a top-5 album and a top-10 single, out-charting KATSEYE on the Hot 100 by some measures. Lara Raj's cameo on "Boys" signals there is no feud.
4. **It is a deliberate pivot from *The Provocateur*.** Capitol, Slatkin and Brady steered her toward a broader, radio-ready sound. Commercially it worked (No. 4 US, No. 2 UK, first Slovak Hot 100 entry and top 10). The open critical question is whether she will get "stranger" again on album two.

## Where sources conflict

- **Label:** Capitol (US) vs Polydor (UK). Both are probably correct for different territories.
- **Hot 100 peaks:** "Ain't in LA" is reported at No. 61 and No. 24 (No. 24 is later). "Nicole Kidman" is reported at No. 6 and No. 9 (No. 6 is the most-cited final peak). A ynetnews claim of No. 11 for "Nicole Kidman" looks outdated.
- **Metacritic:** 68 (9 reviews) vs 69 (8 reviews), depending on the snapshot.
- **Rolling Stone reviewer:** Different sources give different bylines.
- **Unreliable claims:**
  - One review says ADÉLA grew up in communist Czechoslovakia. She was born in 2003, and it was her mother's youth that was under communism.
  - One Wikipedia summary lists "Hitachi" as a single.
  - One Portuguese site says "Ain't in LA" hit No. 1 on the Hot 100.

  I treated all three as errors.
