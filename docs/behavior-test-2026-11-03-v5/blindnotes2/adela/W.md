# ADÉLA – *PRIMA* (2026): a guide

*Researched 3 Nov 2026 from about 45 web searches and page reads. Sources are listed at the end. Where sources disagree, both versions are given.*

## The short version

*PRIMA* is the debut studio album by ADÉLA (Adéla Jergová), a 22-year-old Slovak pop singer and former ballet dancer. It came out on **4 September 2026**: on Capitol Records in the US, with the UK and European press naming Polydor. It has 11 tracks and runs about **31 minutes**. Blake Slatkin and Dylan Brady (one half of 100 gecs) were co-executive producers.

The album debuted at **No. 4 on the Billboard 200** with 56,000 units and **No. 2 in the UK**, and it reportedly reached No. 1 in several countries, including Slovakia. It also produced her first US and UK top-10 single, "Nicole Kidman." Critics were mostly positive but split. Most praised her personality and the closer "Ain't In LA." Several said the album is more polished and mainstream, and more openly modelled on earlier pop, than her edgier 2025 EP *The Provocateur*.

## Who ADÉLA is (context the album depends on)

- **Early life:** Born 27 November 2003 in Petržalka, Bratislava, Slovakia. She started ballet at three, turned professional at 11, and danced the lead in *The Nutcracker* at the Slovak National Theatre at 12. She trained at the Vienna State Ballet from age 14 and at the English National Ballet School in London from 15. When COVID sent her home, she turned to music. She taught herself English from American TV.
- **Dream Academy:** She was a contestant on HYBE x Geffen's *The Debut: Dream Academy* (2023), shown as Netflix's *Pop Star Academy: KATSEYE*. The show formed the girl group KATSEYE. She finished joint last in a fan vote and was eliminated early. She later said contestants "had no control over our own image," and that her first EP channelled her anger about that public experience. Afterwards she stayed in LA, earned her GED while living in her vocal coach's attic, and took any writing session she could get.
- **Solo career before PRIMA:**
  - Her first singles were "Homewrecked" and "Superscar" (2024).
  - "MachineGirl" (2025) was co-produced by Grimes.
  - She signed with Capitol, and per Qobuz also Polydor, in May 2025.
  - *The Provocateur* EP (August 2025, led by "SexOnTheBeat") was gritty, provocative electropop. It was shortlisted for a Slovak Anděl award.
  - She opened for Demi Lovato's North American tour and sold out her first solo shows in London, New York and LA.

## Title and concept

- **"Prima"** has two meanings. It means "first," since this is her debut. It also refers to the *prima ballerina*, the top dancer in a ballet company and the highest rank a woman can reach. Her stated goal: *"I'm trying to be the prima ballerina of pop, and this is my first step to achieve that."*
- **Who named it:** Actor Rachel Sennott (*Bottoms*, *I Love LA*), who is Blake Slatkin's partner. ADÉLA credits her with the name (V Magazine).
- **Mission statement:** *"I want this album to sound exactly like a 22-year-old girl."* She says she "mixed the hard with the soft": vulnerability and swagger, muscular beats and feminine energy. Apple Music describes the album as the pop she loved as a preteen (her taste formed around 2014), seen through a 22-year-old's eyes.
- **Themes in the label copy:** insecurity, toxic relationships, love, loss, identity, femininity, bodily autonomy and friendship.
- **Story arc (Metal Magazine):** a "controlled introduction" that moves from plotting a rise to questioning what the rise has cost. That cost includes homesickness, family obligations and an Eastern European outsider identity in the US market.
- **Her description of her voice (NPR, 19 Sept 2026):** "cheeky" and "uncomfortably honest." She ties that bluntness to being Slovak and Eastern European: "I think we're a little feisty."

## Making it

- **Producers:** Blake Slatkin co-executive produced. His credits include "Stay" (The Kid Laroi and Justin Bieber), "Unholy" (Sam Smith and Kim Petras) and work for SZA and Charli xcx. Dylan Brady of 100 gecs was the other co-executive producer. The Dare co-produced "KGB," and Cashmere Cat produced "I'm The Man."
- **Where:** Much of the album was made at Slatkin's house in LA.
- **Who she listened to:** She says she plays songs to no one until they're finished and relied on a small circle for feedback: herself, Slatkin, Brady, the co-writers and Sennott. Chris Horan is her creative director and stylist.
- **Co-writers named in credits:** Theron Thomas (R. City; "We Can't Stop," "About Damn Time," Jungkook's "Seven"), Billy Walsh (Post Malone's main co-writer), Ali Tamposi and Julia Michaels ("Starving Artist," per a lyrics site).
- **Singles tested live:** "Ain't In LA" wasn't planned as a single. Crowd response when she played it on the Demi Lovato tour convinced the team to release it. "Marijuana" was also played live well before the album, according to an earlier Rolling Stone feature.
- **How "Therapy" was recorded:** Her voice was "completely gone" around Grammy week, so she talked rather than sang. That is why the Auto-Tune is so heavy. The original take ran about 15 minutes ("just rambling") before it was cut down. She calls it "not really even a song" (NPR).

## Track by track

The notes combine ADÉLA's own explanations (Capital FM "Making The Album," NPR, V Magazine) with critics' descriptions.

| # | Track | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **KGB** | **Lead single (17 April 2026).** Co-produced with The Dare, and written with The Dare, Slatkin, Brady, Ali Tamposi and Billy Walsh. Dark humour about Slovakia's Soviet-era history and her upbringing. It casts her as a spy "infiltrating" the American music industry, and works as a reply to online critics who mocked her. The video was shot in the LA Theatre and drew on her ballet training. Its dance break went viral. |
| 2 | **Nicole Kidman** | **The breakout hit.** She watched *Eyes Wide Shut*, wanted "more Nicole in the movie," then went to a Golden Globes party in a tight corset and thought, "I feel like Nicole Kidman right now." Produced by Slatkin and Brady, with sped-up soul samples. Hannah Lux Davis directed the video, which nods to *Eyes Wide Shut*, *Moulin Rouge!*, *Birth*, *Babygirl* and Kidman's AMC ad. |
| 3 | **I'm The Man** | Produced by Cashmere Cat. A song about gender roles: she out-earns most men her age and wants a partner secure enough to let "the woman in the relationship" be the boss. |
| 4 | **Boys** | A 2000s club track with Britney and Neptunes/Missy Elliott touches. It's about men who hover near women on nights out but never approach. It includes comic spoken parts by **Lara Raj of KATSEYE**, her former Dream Academy rival. Wonderland says her live choreography recalls Britney's 2001 VMAs performance. |
| 5 | **Red Bottoms** | **Second single (12 June 2026).** Emma Drew Berson directed the video, also shot in the LA Theatre. A worn-out red Louboutin sole stands for a toxic, draining relationship. The song is about perfectionism and her fear that a partner will see her "ugly" side; she says she opens up more easily to a crowd than to one person. Critics called it some of her strongest writing, and her headline tour took its name. |
| 6 | **Hitachi** | A piano ballad. It started as a deliberately silly upbeat song after Slatkin mentioned the Hitachi wand vibrator, then became "a realer song about troubles in your relationship," about fading intimacy. |
| 7 | **Fantasize** | A follow-up to "Hitachi": if you're unsatisfied, talk, teach each other, or leave, because "life is too short." It has brassy, trombone-heavy, '90s-flavoured production. |
| 8 | **Starving Artist** | Came from a session about an ex who was "good in bed but broke." Reviewers heard class detail sharpening the innuendo, and one critic heard a Gwen Stefani-ish outro. |
| 9 | **Marijuana** | The shortest track, under two minutes, with a nostalgic 2015-era hyperpop/2010s feel. She notes she doesn't use drugs at all. Ringtone called it the best album cut, while Rolling Stone found the lyrics weak. |
| 10 | **Therapy** | The most exposed and experimental track: spoken, heavily Auto-Tuned, over sparse keys. It covers her father, guilt over missing her little sister growing up ("I wish I could have been there for her more"), not yet being able to move her mother closer, and frustration with men controlling her career. It splits critics: "the most vulnerable" for some, "difficult to listen to" for others. |
| 11 | **Ain't In LA** | **Third single (23 July 2026), and the closer.** Written with Theron Thomas, Billy Walsh, Brady and Slatkin. It was inspired by a photo of her mother at 18 in front of Western pop posters (Madonna, Marilyn Monroe, Keanu Reeves) that her grandfather had smuggled in from West Germany under communism. Her mother once planned to move to LA but got pregnant instead. The song is dedicated to her mother "and the billion other bitches who are probably cooler than me," meaning talented girls in overlooked cities. "LA is what it is because of all the immigrants, just like myself." It uses booming 808s and a chopped-and-screwed intro that echoes "We Can't Stop," with uncredited vocals from Theron Thomas. Fred Gervais shot the video in Bratislava, with her mother Mária and sister Stela. Most critics call it the album's best song. |

## Commercial performance

**United States**
- **Billboard 200:** debuted at No. 4 with about 56,000 equivalent units, against forecasts of 40–45,000. That breaks down as about 31,000 pure sales and about 26 million on-demand streams.
- **Other Billboard album charts:** No. 1 on Top Album Sales and on Vinyl Albums.
- **Spotify:** No. 1 debut album in the US and globally.

**United Kingdom**
- **Official Albums Chart:** entered at No. 2 (chart dated 17 September 2026) and spent 6 weeks on the chart, to 22 October.
- **Other UK and Irish charts:** No. 1 in Scotland, No. 2 on the Sales and Physical charts, No. 3 on Vinyl, No. 5 in Ireland.

**Elsewhere**
- **Slovakia and the Netherlands:** reported No. 1 (Time Out, Qobuz, Wikipedia). Qobuz says No. 1 in four countries. Wikipedia also lists Belgium, which I could not confirm independently.
- **Top 10s:** in the UK and Australia, and reportedly several other countries.

## Cultural moment and promotion

- **Nicole Kidman's response:** At the London premiere of *Practical Magic 2* she said, "I absolutely know who she is… I'm going to go and see her" in Nashville. Her **first-ever TikTok post** (late August 2026) used "Ain't In LA" as its audio, not "Nicole Kidman."
- **The lip-sync trend:** "Nicole Kidman" became a lip-sync trend. Kidman, Dua Lipa, Pedro Pascal and Kristen Wiig did it together at Chanel's Spring/Summer 2027 show in Paris, and Madonna joined in on TikTok.
- **TV and radio:** Her US late-night debut was *The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon* on 22 October 2026, with a medley of "Nicole Kidman" and "Ain't In LA." She also appeared on Elton John's *Rocket Hour*.
- **Touring:** She played Lollapalooza on 2 August and London's HERE at Outernet on 25 August. The **Red Bottoms Tour** played North America from 9 September to 9 October, then Mexico City on 15–16 October; the North American dates sold out in minutes. A UK and Europe leg runs January–February 2027 (Prague, Dublin, Manchester, Glasgow, London ×2, Paris, Cologne, Brussels, Utrecht, Copenhagen, Stockholm, Oslo, Berlin, Warsaw). KATSEYE members came to the LA show.
- **Streaming:** By autumn she reportedly passed KATSEYE in Spotify monthly listeners (about 34.7M vs about 33.9M, per IBTimes).

## Critical reception

Overall the reviews are favourable but mixed. A student paper quotes a Metacritic score of **68 from 9 reviews**, with Variety among the most positive and Pitchfork among the most sceptical. I could not reach Metacritic or Pitchfork to verify this.

| Outlet | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Clash** (Robin Murray) | **8/10**. "A pop debut with palpable star quality"; "an unrelenting debut, a coherent run of pop ambition." Singles out "Hitachi" as one of her strongest vocals. |
| **Rolling Stone** (Larisha Paul) | Positive, no score. Headline: "Adéla's Risk-Taking Pays Off on *Prima*"; "an ambitious debut fueled by determination, pop potential, and a quick-witted perspective." Picks: "Nicole Kidman," "Boys," "Red Bottoms," "Therapy," "Ain't In LA." Weaker: "KGB" (insistent), "Starving Artist" (could have been cut). Also says "Predictable doesn't really work for her." |
| **Ratings Game Music** | **B+**. Favourites: "KGB," "Starving Artist." |
| **The Post (Athens, OH)** | **3.5/5**. "Few duds" but "an unfortunate step back from her EP" that "wears its influences too heavily on its sleeves." |
| **Pipe Dream (Binghamton)** | **4/5**. Confident and catchy, but tamer than *The Provocateur*. Notes fan frustration at the more mainstream sound. |
| **The Line of Best Fit** (Douglas Jardim) | **6/10**. "Plays dress-up with pop history." Its "showy peaks are spoiled by stock slow jams," in "a formulaic approach that is both aiding her popularity and interfering with her artistic development." "Ain't In LA" is the "saving grace." |
| **Sputnik Music** | **3.0**. Too many mid-tempo songs and ballads, "more misses than hits," but a strong close. |
| **Ringtone** | Lukewarm: "music to shop to," too indebted to Ariana Grande, "pulling her punches." But "when it gets good, God, it's good." |

**Common themes in the reviews**
- **What critics praise:**
  - her personality, wit and star presence
  - the run of "Nicole Kidman," "Red Bottoms," "Therapy" and especially "Ain't In LA"
  - the Slovak and post-communist identity running through "KGB," "Therapy" and "Ain't In LA"
- **What critics criticise:**
  - she trades the EP's weirdness for polished, reference-heavy pop (Britney, Ariana, Gwen Stefani, Miley, mid-2010s hip-hop pop)
  - too many ballads
  - the best material is bunched at the end
- **Other praise:** The label quotes The Atlantic comparing her penchant for minor chords and meta-commentary to early Lady Gaga.

## How to listen to it

- **Start with the three hits:** "Ain't In LA," "Nicole Kidman" and "Red Bottoms."
- **Hear it as a story:** The album runs from "KGB" (myth-making ambition) through a middle stretch of sex and relationships ("Hitachi" → "Fantasize" → "Starving Artist" plays as a mini-trilogy) to the confessional, nearly unsung "Therapy," and then the release of "Ain't In LA." That ending ties her ambition back to her mother and to Bratislava.
- **Hear the reference points:** It is a former ballerina's "first step" toward being pop's *prima*, consciously built from the pop she grew up on. That reading explains both the praise (self-aware, witty, star-making) and the main criticism (derivative, safer than the EP).
