# Judge: Mon Rovîa / Bloodline

**Ranking: P > Q > S > R**

Judged from the asker's side: which answer leaves me understanding who he is, how the album works, why it sounds the way it does, and the context around it, and which one reads best. I read all four in full and did not check anything on the web, so factual notes below are about internal consistency and cross-answer disagreement only.

## 1. P
The best answer for understanding. It explains *why* the album sounds as it does: the foster brothers' Fleet Foxes/Bon Iver records, the lockdown ukulele, the rap-to-folk shift, and the 1920s "hillbilly" vs "race" record split that made him believe folk was "white". It also gives the soft-sound/harrowing-lyrics tension as the album's core and is careful to mark its own inferences (Campbell return reading, Rhiannon Giddens parallel, the meaning of the song titles) and the uncertain child-soldier claim. The prose flows well. Weaknesses: there is no track list or quick listening guide, and a few interpretive stretches (the "retraces that route in reverse" loop, the Joseph/Dolly reading) take up space, though they are labelled as such.

## 2. Q
Close to P, with a similar "how he got here explains the sound" structure. It adds useful specifics: his own stated reason for the soft sound ("break down people's edges"), that the Nick Drake comparison is a resemblance and not an influence, instrument-by-track texture, and the Graceland outsider-vs-insider contrast. The closing "What is his own" section is a sharp synthesis. Weaknesses: "Freed Black Americans founded Liberia" overstates things (the colony was organised by the American Colonization Society, and P puts it more accurately). It calls *Bloodline* his "first full-length album" while also calling *Dark Continent* a 2021 "album" without reconciling the two. A few claims rest on one source (Tracy Chapman, the Dazed Palestine detail), though it flags the Chapman one.

## 3. S
The most complete reference sheet: detailed timeline, singles, charts, producer, pre-saves, a track-by-track grouped into four life stages, and an honest caveats section (press-release-sourced quotes, the single-source Bella Hadid story). The grouped track list and the short "How to think about it" section give some sense of the album's arc. Weaknesses: it is mostly *what* and very little *why*. The sound and the Afro-Appalachian idea get one bullet each, so I would come away knowing facts but not really understanding the music. It names Andre Samuel and Daylight as producers, while P and Q both say Cooper Holzman in Los Angeles. One side is wrong or incomplete, and S doesn't acknowledge the conflict.

## 4. R
Clean and readable. It has the same timeline and FLOOD track-by-track as S and a useful listening suggestion. It is honest about gaps (adoption age, producer and chart position unknown, promo figures). Weaknesses: it is the thinnest on explanation. There is almost nothing on why the sound pairs gentle folk with war memories, on what "Afro-Appalachian" means historically, or on his rap/R&B past as an influence. It overlaps heavily with S but has fewer details and less framing. The one-line track summaries are mostly paraphrases of his own promo notes.

## Things that look wrong, conflicting or overclaimed
- **Producer conflict:** P and Q say Cooper Holzman (LA); S says Andre Samuel and Daylight (Native Instruments interview); R says it couldn't find the credits. These can't all be complete.
- **Track 7 spelling:** P and Q write "Old Fort Steel Trail"; R and S write "Old Fort Steele Trail".
- **Title track:** P says it is "built on found sound from a church service" and Q says it opens with a church recording, while R says it is "sung with a choir". These may be compatible.
- **Liberia's founding (Q):** "Freed Black Americans founded Liberia" is an overstatement.
- **Child soldier:** R and S repeat that the adoption saved him from being a child soldier. Only P flags the conflicting account (NME vs a local profile).
- **Debut vs Dark Continent:** P and S handle this ambiguity explicitly; Q states both claims without reconciling them.
- **Promo numbers:** streaming and TikTok figures (200M+, about 1B) come from label copy, as R and S note. P's "close to a billion views" is stated without that caveat.
