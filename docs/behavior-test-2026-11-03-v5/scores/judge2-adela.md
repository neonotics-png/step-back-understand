# Judge 2: ADÉLA, PRIMA

**Ranking: Y > X > Z > W**

## 1. Y (~1,400 words)
Y is built around "why it is this way." It names the three forces behind the record (two producers pulling in opposite directions, the 2007–2015 pop it aims at, and the Bratislava outsider view) and ties the credits, her own quotes (the Paper interview about wanting "Julia Michaels level" songs and being tired of provocation for its own sake) and the split reception into one causal story. Of the four, it gives the most understanding per minute of reading, and it closes with a useful frame (the Olivia Rodrigo/SOUR comparison). Weaknesses: there is little track-level detail. "Julia Michaels co-wrote most of the record" looks overclaimed, since the other answers credit her on only a few songs. "First contestant eliminated" is sharper than the "eliminated early / joint last" in the other answers. Brady's work on the EP is asserted but not shown.

## 2. X (~1,200 words)
X is the tightest explanatory answer. Its sound section is especially good: Slatkin supplies the structure and toplines while Brady supplies texture and low end, which it backs with credits and which explains why the album is less hyperpop than the EP. Its KGB-to-"Ain't in LA" arc ("from wanting in to deciding you don't have to move there") is the clearest single takeaway of the four, and it honestly labels its own inference. Weaknesses: it says "Ain't in LA" peaked at No. 61, which is likely outdated, since W and Z report a later No. 24. It reduces "Therapy" to being about her sister, while others say it also covers her father and mother. A few credit claims are unhedged and conflict with the other answers: "co-produced every track," though others credit Cashmere Cat with "I'm The Man," and "engineered all of them."

## 3. Z (~2,700 words)
Z is the strongest reference document. It has a quick-facts table, a track-by-track with durations, credits and samples, the widest critical table (Guardian, Pitchfork 5.7, NME, Slant, Variety, Billboard), and a careful "where sources conflict / unreliable claims" section that shows good judgment. Its "How to make sense of it" section gives four solid interpretive points, but they come at the end of a long fact dump, so the understanding per minute is much lower than in Y or X. Some claims are mildly overclaimed: the tour "sold out its North American and European legs," and the KATSEYE out-charting "by some measures" is vague. Bonus-track and alternate-lyric trivia adds length without adding insight.

## 4. W (~2,900 words)
W is thorough and well sourced, with good track-by-track notes that carry her own explanations (the "Therapy" recording story, the origin of "Ain't In LA") and honest flags about what it could not verify. But it reads as an encyclopedic dossier. Tour city lists, Spotify listener counts and promo minutiae take up reading time without explaining the album, and the interpretive payoff ("How to listen to it") is short and arrives late. Possible errors or weak points: it says Rolling Stone gave "no score" (Z reports 3.5/5), it couldn't confirm the Pitchfork score or Metacritic, and its critics table leans on student and blog outlets while missing major reviews that Z covers.

## Cross-answer flags
- The Hot 100 peak of "Ain't in LA": X says 61, while W and Z say a later 24. X is likely stale.
- The Rolling Stone score: W says unscored, Z says 3.5/5.
- The subject of "Therapy": X says her sister only, Y says especially her father, W and Z say family broadly.
- The No. 1 countries, Belgium/Flanders and Scotland, rest mainly on Wikipedia. W is the only answer that flags this as unconfirmed.
