# Judge: ADÉLA, PRIMA

**Ranking: Q > P > S > R**

Judged from the asker's side ("help me understand PRIMA"): does the answer explain what the album is, why it sounds the way it does, and the context behind it, and does it read well? I read only the four files and did not check anything on the web, so where answers disagree I flag the disagreement and don't rule on which is right.

## 1. Q
Best at explaining the album. It ties the sound to evidence from the credits: Slatkin co-wrote 10 of the 11 songs and engineered all of them, Serban Ghenea mixed the first five, and Brady is credited mostly for synths, bass and drums, which supports "hooks first, noise as decoration." It also links the 2007-2015 sound to the albums she names and to specific co-writers (Theron Thomas, Uffie, The Dare). The themes form a clear arc: America as an imported dream, "KGB" as the spy who wants in, "Ain't in LA" as deciding she doesn't have to move. It is honest about what is its own reading ("that reading comes from me") and about sources disagreeing (the sister's age, the Hot 100 peak). Weaknesses: it is dense with names, and it gives less of a song-by-song picture than S.

## 2. P
Reads the best of the four. It is built on the same "why" structure: two producers pulling in opposite directions, the decade it aims at, the ballet frame, and an outsider's view of American pop. The Paper quotes about Julia Michaels and moving away from the EP explain the change in sound well, and the Olivia Rodrigo/SOUR comparison is a useful closing frame. Possible errors and overclaims:
- It says "Therapy" is about "her family and especially her father." Q and S both say it is about her sister.
- "Julia Michaels co-wrote most of the record" is not supported by the other answers, which give Slatkin as the main co-writer.
- It says Theron Thomas "supplies the slowed, chopped vocal intro." Q says he raps the refrain.
- It treats Rolling Stone as negative ("Predictable doesn't really work for her"). S reports the same review's headline as positive ("Risk-Taking Pays Off").

## 3. S
The most complete reference: a quick-facts table, the KATSEYE villain-edit backstory, a track-by-track guide, a review table, touring, and the chart-peak disagreement handled carefully. It is useful for looking things up, but it explains less of why the album is the way it is. Its themes are mostly a list, and the producer split is only one line. Possible errors: it calls Rolling Stone positive, which conflicts with P and Q, and it lists only six reviews, with no Metacritic score or Pitchfork. The "Nicole Kidman" trend list (Madonna, a Chanel lip-sync with Pedro Pascal, Dua Lipa and others) is very specific and not hedged. Some of it (tour dates, venues, Spotify listener comparisons) is padding for someone trying to understand the album.

## 4. R
A clean, accurate-looking fact sheet: quick facts, background, tracklist, the story behind "Ain't In LA," charts and a few reviews. Good hedging on the label and on chart positions. But it mostly lists things instead of explaining them. The sound section is four thin bullets, the themes are a list of nouns, and nothing connects the background (ballet, Dream Academy, growing up in Bratislava) to the songs. It says the least about what the album means, so a reader comes away knowing facts about it rather than understanding it.
