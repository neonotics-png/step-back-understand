# ADÉLA and PRIMA: Comprehension Quiz

Answer each question in 2-4 sentences.

1. The opening track and lead single "KGB" casts ADÉLA in a specific persona. What is that persona, what does it say about her position as a newcomer in American pop, and how did she handle the Soviet imagery it draws on?

2. How does the closing track "Ain't In LA" work? Explain what it is drawn from, what message it carries, and how it shifts the album's story of ambition aimed at Los Angeles.

3. ADÉLA has described PRIMA as mixing "the hard with the soft." What did she mean, and how does that contrast show up across the album? Use one upbeat track and "Therapy" as examples.

4. Why is the album called PRIMA, and why does it aim at polished, mainstream pop rather than the stranger electropop of her earlier EP? Draw on what she has said about her background and goals.

5. Why is PRIMA so frank, cheeky and provocative about sex and the body (for example "Hitachi," "Boys" and "I'm the Man")? Explain the personal and artistic reasons she has given for this approach.

6. Trace the path that led to PRIMA, from her reality-TV beginnings to her first EP. How did that path shape her public image, and how does PRIMA relate to the EP that came before it?

7. How was PRIMA received by critics, and what was the main point of disagreement between its admirers and its skeptics? Note any song that both sides tended to agree on.

8. A friend knows ADÉLA only as the "villain" from Netflix's Pop Star Academy: KATSEYE and expects PRIMA to be a bitter revenge record aimed at the group that rejected her. What would they actually find on the album, and why does that fit how ADÉLA has handled that part of her history?
