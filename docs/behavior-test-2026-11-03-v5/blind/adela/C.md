# ADÉLA *PRIMA*: quiz answers (from memory only)

**1. "KGB"**
I only know that "KGB" is the opener and was released as a single before the album was announced. Clash called it "celebratory and subversive," and Sputnik said the album never matches its raunchy, catchy chorus. I didn't read anything about the persona it casts her in, what it says about her as a newcomer to American pop, or how she handled the Soviet imagery, so I can't answer that part.

**2. "Ain't In LA"**
She wrote it after a trip home to Bratislava, prompted by a photo of her mother at 18 posing in front of a wall of Western pop-culture images. In her words, she wrote it "for her and the billion other bitches who are probably cooler than me." Fred Gervais shot the video in Bratislava and filled it with LA and Hollywood references, and the song is built on a pitched-down vocal sample in a mid-2010s pop/hip-hop style. My read is that closing on it turns the album's dream of LA back toward home: the cool and the ambition were already in Bratislava. That last point is my interpretation, not something I saw her say. It was also her first Billboard Hot 100 entry, peaking at No. 61.

**3. "The hard with the soft"**
She said she mixed vulnerable moments with swagger, and muscular beats with feminine energy, so the record would "sound exactly like a 22-year-old girl." On the hard side, "Boys" is a club stomper that Clash compared to Missy Elliott and the Neptunes. I didn't learn anything specific about "Therapy" beyond its place in the tracklist (track 10), so I can't describe how it shows the soft side. Critics did note the album has more mid-tempo songs and ballads than her earlier work.

**4. Why PRIMA, and why polished pop**
"PRIMA means 'first,'" since it's her debut. It also refers to the *prima ballerina*, the lead dancer of a company, because she trained in ballet at the Vienna State Ballet and the English National Ballet School. She said, "I'm trying to be the prima ballerina of pop, and this is my first step." That goal of being the top pop star helps explain the more mainstream polish, with Blake Slatkin co-executive producing alongside Dylan Brady. I didn't find her own explanation of why she moved away from the stranger electropop of *The Provocateur*; critics simply noted the shift.

**5. Sex and the body**
I don't know. My research didn't turn up any personal or artistic reasons she has given for the album's frank approach to sex, beyond the general "swagger" and "feminine energy" framing. Clash did single out "Hitachi" as one of her strongest vocals and "I'm The Man" (produced by Cashmere Cat) as the song where she asserts herself.

**6. The path to PRIMA**
In 2023 she was on *Dream Academy*, the HYBE x Geffen show that formed KATSEYE, and was eliminated early, joint last. Netflix's 2024 docuseries *Pop Star Academy: KATSEYE* then showed that run to a large audience. After that came the 2024 singles "Homewrecked" and "Superscar," then the Grimes co-produced "Machine Girl" (2025), a Capitol deal in May 2025, and the August 2025 EP *The Provocateur*, which included "SexOnTheBeat" with a Christina Aguilera cameo. The EP built a provocative, edgy image. PRIMA keeps that attitude but is glossier and more mainstream, and some critics found it weaker than the EP.

**7. Critical reception**
Reviews were mixed to positive: Clash 8/10, the Daily Illini 9/10, The Line of Best Fit 6/10 and Sputnik 3.0/5. Fans of the album praised her charisma, the coherent sequencing and the glossy club pop. Skeptics thought it was less distinctive than *The Provocateur*, heavy on mid-tempo songs and ballads, and sometimes thin lyrically. "Ain't In LA" was the most widely praised song, including by Sputnik ("radio-ready anthem").

**8. The "villain" expectation**
I can't fully answer this. I learned that she was eliminated on *Dream Academy* and that the press has asked her about "being a villain." I didn't read anything about how she handles that history, and nothing I found says the album targets KATSEYE. From what I did learn, the album's subjects are her own ambition, insecurities, relationships, identity and home and family, not revenge.
