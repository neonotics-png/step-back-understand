# ADÉLA, PRIMA: quiz answers (from memory, no lookups)

**1. "KGB"**
The song casts ADÉLA as a spy from the former Eastern Bloc who has infiltrated the American pop industry. The title points to the Soviet security agency, and the spy image stands for her relentless, drilled-in training: the outsider who studied American pop, from Disney Channel onward, until she could slip inside it. The tone is tongue-in-cheek bravado. W Magazine reported that the video ends with a disclaimer saying the imagery acknowledges where she comes from, does not endorse anything, and that she stands with Ukraine. That was the only source I found for the disclaimer, and I saw no reporting of a wider controversy.

**2. "Ain't In LA"**
The song comes from a photo of her mother at 18 posing in front of Western pop posters. Her grandfather, who worked in West German mines, had smuggled them into communist Czechoslovakia. The message is that talented, beautiful, interesting girls exist outside LA and often never "get their flowers." She called it a song "for my mom and the billion other bitches who are probably cooler than me." The video was shot in Bratislava and is full of LA and Hollywood references. Coming after an album about chasing American stardom, the closer turns that ambition around: it honours home and people who never left, instead of treating LA as the only place that counts.

**3. "The hard with the soft"**
She said she wanted the album to "sound exactly like a 22-year-old girl," combining hardness and softness, vulnerability and confidence, "pounding beats and feminine energy." On the hard side, "KGB" and "Boys" are brash, swaggering dance-pop and club tracks with heavy production and Neptunes-style bounce. On the soft side, "Therapy" is a quiet, distorted, whispery ballad about older-sister guilt over not being there for her younger sister while chasing a career abroad. The album keeps switching between bangers and confessional ballads such as "Red Bottoms" and "Hitachi."

**4. The title and the mainstream pivot**
"PRIMA" means "first," which fits a debut. It also refers to the prima ballerina, the top soloist in a ballet company. She trained in ballet from age three through the Vienna State Ballet and the English National Ballet School, and says she is "trying to be the prima ballerina of pop." On the polish: she grew up romanticizing American pop, learning English from Disney Channel, and cites *My Everything*, *Bangerz* and Beyoncé's self-titled album as formative. She calls herself a "student of pop" and a "showgirl at heart" who wants to play arenas, so a glossy, mainstream, 2010s-nostalgic record with Blake Slatkin and Dylan Brady fits that goal. I don't recall her explicitly explaining why she moved away from the EP's stranger sound. That framing comes mostly from critics and fans.

**5. Sex and the body**
From what I read:
- "Hitachi," named for the vibrator, began as an upbeat song and became "a realer song about troubles in your relationship," about fading intimacy.
- "I'm the Man" flips gender roles. In her words, she needs men "comfortable with themselves enough to be down to be the baby girl."
- "Boys" lampoons men who hover around women on nights out.

The label framed this as sex-positive self-expression and challenging gender and power roles. Since *The Provocateur*, provocation has been part of her image, which The FADER described as "a weapon used to seize control of her story." Beyond those points I don't have a detailed personal explanation from her of why she writes so frankly, so I won't invent one.

**6. The path to PRIMA**
- **2023:** She was one of 20 finalists on HYBE x Geffen's *The Debut: Dream Academy*, which formed KATSEYE, and was cut in the first elimination round.
- **2024:** Netflix's *Pop Star Academy: KATSEYE* gave her a "villain edit." An executive's line, "We see Adéla as a solo artist," became her fan tagline.
- **2024–2025:** She went independent with "Homewrecked" (Sept 2024) and "Superscar," then "Machine Girl," co-produced by Grimes. She signed with Capitol in 2025 and released the abrasive industrial-pop EP *The Provocateur* in August 2025.

That path gave her the image of a defiant, provocative underdog with pink hair. *PRIMA* keeps the attitude and visual boldness. It trades the EP's grit and hyperpop weirdness for a broader, more personal, radio-ready pop sound, and many critics and fans framed it as a step away from the EP.

**7. Critical reception**
Reviews were favorable but split. Metacritic sits around 68–69. Clash (8/10), Variety, Billboard and the Sydney Morning Herald were enthusiastic. Rolling Stone, NME and Slant gave it 3.5/5, the Guardian 3/5, The Line of Best Fit 6/10 and Pitchfork 5.7/10. Admirers heard star quality, wit, tight sequencing and real vulnerability. Skeptics heard a safer, derivative pastiche of Britney, Ariana and 2010s pop that "pales in comparison" to *The Provocateur*. Both sides broadly agreed on the closer "Ain't In LA," called the "saving grace" even by the harshest reviews (Line of Best Fit, Ringtone, Sputnik). "Nicole Kidman" was also widely liked, though not universally.

**8. The "revenge record" expectation**
They wouldn't find a revenge record. As far as I saw, no song attacks KATSEYE. The album is about her own ambition, relationships, family and home. The only direct nod is friendly: KATSEYE's Lara Raj gives uncredited spoken interludes on "Boys," and ADÉLA stayed close with several former contestants. That fits how she has handled the history. She treated the elimination and villain edit as fuel for going solo, and embraced the "solo artist" line as a tagline instead of nursing a grudge. Her success, with a top-5 album and a top-10 single, does the talking. I did see a headline about resurfaced comments she made about KATSEYE's Manon, but I didn't read it, so I can't characterize it.
