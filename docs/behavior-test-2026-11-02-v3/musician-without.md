# Why Khruangbin sounds like Khruangbin

Khruangbin is a Houston trio formed in 2010: Mark Speer (guitar), Laura Lee Ochoa (bass) and Donald "DJ" Johnson (drums). The short version of why they sound the way they do is that the sound is not an aesthetic they chose so much as the residue of three specific biographies, one city, one building, and a small set of rules they agreed on early. Speer summed the rules up himself: "Funky drums, dub bass, melodic guitar, those are the only rules." Everything below is an attempt to show where each of those three rules came from, and why the songs mostly have no words.

A note on method: this is assembled from interviews and features (linked throughout), not from my own listening. Where sources disagree on dates, I say so.

---

## 1. The three people

### Mark Speer: a Houston crate-digger with a gospel day job

Speer is the band's musical engine and the person most responsible for its palette. Several threads in his background matter:

- **Hip-hop first, rock later.** He told Premier Guitar he was drawn to hip-hop as a kid (he names Grandmaster Flash's "The Message") and initially disliked rock. He wanted to play drums, could not afford a kit, so played bass, and only learned guitar later to play "big jazz chords, like fusion-jazz type chords." A guitarist who started as a bassist thinking like a drummer explains a lot about a band in which the guitar rarely strums and the groove leads. ([Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/khruangbins-mark-speer-addicted-to-reverb))
- **Seeing P-Funk at 12.** He saw Parliament-Funkadelic at Texas Southern University in 1992 and says that is when he decided he wanted to perform; he cites George Clinton's "free your mind and your ass will follow" as a philosophy. ([Houston Press](https://www.houstonpress.com/?p=4292))
- **The world-music bin.** He grew up near a Half Price Books and gravitated to the world-music vinyl section. ([Houston Press](https://www.houstonpress.com/?p=4292)) Later he dug through Houston's South Asian shopping district (now officially the Mahatma Gandhi District), buying records whose sleeves "are all written in Hindi or one of the other scripts, so I don't understand what any of it says." ([Rolling Stone India](https://rollingstoneindia.com/?p=94563))
- **Ten years in a megachurch band.** Around the end of the 1990s he started playing gospel, on bass, learning songs on the fly by watching the organist's feet. ([NPR via LAist](https://laist.com/news/npr-news/after-texas-sun-leon-bridges-and-khruangbin-seek-solace-on-texas-moon)) By 2004 he was guitarist in Pastor Rudy Rasmus's band at St. John's Methodist Church in downtown Houston, playing most Sundays for roughly 3,000 people, mostly gospel, jazz and R&B *instrumentals*. ([Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-con-todo-el-mundo-interview), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khruangbin)) He has said he finds a lot of gospel repetitive but "the good stuff will move you to tears," and that he likes "the mystery of it," music left open to interpretation. ([LAist/NPR](https://laist.com/news/npr-news/after-texas-sun-leon-bridges-and-khruangbin-seek-solace-on-texas-moon))

### DJ Johnson: church organist and Houston rap producer

Johnson is the reason the drums are the way they are, and his background is the most purely Houston of the three.

- He grew up playing in church from around age seven, moved to organ in his late teens, and as of 2019 still played Hammond B3 in his church's gospel band. He and Speer met in the St. John's band, where Johnson was the organist, not the drummer. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khruangbin), [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/features/artist-features/how-laura-lee-found-her-sound)) Lee knew him as a keyboard player and was surprised he drummed at all; Speer's pitch was simply that he would "play the way we want." ([Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/features/artist-features/how-laura-lee-found-her-sound))
- He is one half of Beanz N Kornbread, a Houston hip-hop production duo with credits for Slim Thug, Paul Wall, Z-Ro and Chamillionaire. "Pretty much if they're from Houston, we've worked with them." ([Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-con-todo-el-mundo-interview), [Bandcamp Daily, Mordechai](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-mordechai-interview))
- The brief when he joined was explicit: lay down simple breakbeats under the guitar and bass. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khruangbin)) He plays a small jazz-sized kit, avoids fills and solos, and says "the music tells you what it needs." ([Live for Live Music](https://liveforlivemusic.com/features/khruangbin-interview-2019/)) His stated foundations are James Brown's drummers Clyde Stubblefield and Jabo Starks, plus hip-hop from both coasts and the South. ([Austin Chronicle](https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/khruangbin-slows-it-down-houston-style-12102184))

A church-trained timekeeper who makes beats for a living is going to play like a sampled break: steady, unshowy, pocket over flash. That is the "funky drums" rule.

### Laura Lee: the beginner who learned bass from dub records

Lee's story is the one that most directly explains the bass-led grooves, because she learned the instrument *for* this band, from a very specific diet.

- She was a third-grade math teacher who had dabbled in piano and guitar. She met Speer through friends (Wikipedia says 2007; Bandcamp says 2009) when she walked in on him watching a film about Afghan music; they bonded over Middle Eastern music and architecture (she had studied Middle Eastern art history). ([Jambands](https://jambands.com/?p=10866), [Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-con-todo-el-mundo-interview), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khruangbin))
- Speer told her, while she was learning piano with him, to follow the bass line. "Then I picked one up, and it weirdly made sense to me." He gave her records to learn from and bought her an SX J-style bass as a graduation gift; she still plays it, with the original flatwound strings. ([Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/features/artist-features/how-laura-lee-found-her-sound), [XLR8R](https://xlr8r.com/features/bubblin-up-khruangbin/))
- What she learned from, in her own words: "I learned by playing along to the Roots Radics, Serge Gainsbourg, and classic Motown records." Her feel comes from dub reggae; her tone model is the session players on Gainsbourg's records; James Jamerson gave her a love of pentatonics. ([Laura Lee interview, emilyelhaj.com](https://www.emilyelhaj.com/blog/laura-lee-interview)) The band's own statement for their dub album says she learned bass listening to *Scientist Wins the World Cup*, and that Scientist's mixes, "with the emphasis on space and texture," create "the feeling of frozen time." ([Line of Best Fit](https://thelineofbestfit.com/news/latest-news/khruangbin-to-release-dub-version-of-con-todo-el-mundo))
- She is self-consciously untrained and treats that as a feature. She does not use all four left-hand fingers ("I attach two of my fingers together"), compares it to Jamerson's one-finger method, and says the limitation makes her playing "fun and bouncy." ([Guitar World via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/khruangbin-laura-lee-explains-her-105115803.html)) Kamasi Washington's band told her she was very "pocket"; she says she has "no chops, just pocket." ([Live for Live Music](https://liveforlivemusic.com/features/khruangbin-interview-2019/))

Dub bass is, by design, the lead instrument: simple, repetitive, melodic, mixed loud, with the drums locked underneath and everything else floating in echo above. A bassist who learned entirely from Roots Radics and Scientist records, and who was never trained to play busier, is going to write exactly the kind of lines Khruangbin is known for. That is the "dub bass" rule.

## 2. The city: why Houston specifically

The band is emphatic that Houston is not incidental. Johnson: "Our playing, our vocabulary, the way that we are, everything is influenced by Houston." ([Austin Chronicle](https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/khruangbin-slows-it-down-houston-style-12102184)) Four things about the city show up in the music.

**1. The slow tempo is a DJ Screw inheritance.** Houston's defining musical export of the '90s was chopped-and-screwed hip-hop, slowed down to a codeine crawl. Johnson says he put subtle nods to DJ Screw on the first two albums and connects Screw's sound to a "psychedelic and kaleidoscopic" culture. Speer says he dislikes sped-up music on principle: "I'm from Houston, we *have* to slow it down!" Lee describes the Houston feel as "swimming through molasses" and says nobody in the city is in a rush. ([Austin Chronicle](https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/khruangbin-slows-it-down-houston-style-12102184)) Her shorthand for the band: "We're instrumental, we're from Houston." ([Austin Chronicle, ACL](https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/acl-fest-interview-khruangbin-11675045/))

**2. The gospel church as a musicians' commons.** St. John's drew players from across the city (The Forty-Five notes Beyoncé grew up singing there) and gave Speer and Johnson a decade of playing together every week, often instrumentally, in a large room. ([The Forty-Five](https://thefortyfive.com/?p=17098), [Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-con-todo-el-mundo-interview)) Lee has pointed to the "gospel church sound" as something audible on the records. ([Line of Best Fit](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/features/interviews/khruangbin-in-conversation))

**3. Houston's immigrant neighborhoods as a record collection.** Speer: "The things that I grew up hearing in Houston will find their way into what I play... That's that Indian influence. That's that West African influence, that's that northern Mexican influence." He also lists zydeco, French Creole music and Houston's large East Asian population as "a huge reason why we sound the way we do." ([Rolling Stone India](https://rollingstoneindia.com/?p=94563)) Lee's grandfather in Brownsville, who asked her "¿Cómo me quieres?" until she answered "con todo el mundo," gave the second album its title. ([Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-con-todo-el-mundo-interview))

**4. Houston's hip-hop economy.** Johnson's production career means the band's drummer was already making beats for the city's rappers; a Run-DMC "Peter Piper"-style breakdown and the "Apache" break turn up in "Maria También" by the band's own account. ([Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-con-todo-el-mundo-interview), [Austin Chronicle](https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/khruangbin-slows-it-down-houston-style-12102184))

## 3. The records: Thai cassettes, Iranian pop, Jamaican dub

### Thai funk, via a blog

The band's origin myth is accurate as far as the sources go: Speer found Monrakplengthai, a blog of digitized 1960s–70s Thai cassettes (luk thung, mor lam, and the Western-influenced "shadow music" of bands like the Impossibles). He played the tapes at home while Lee cooked and studied Thai with a bootleg Rosetta Stone; the band's name, "airplane" in Thai, came from those lessons. They listened to the cassettes on the 90-minute drive out to the barn where they rehearsed. ([XLR8R](https://xlr8r.com/features/bubblin-up-khruangbin/), [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/khruangbins-mark-speer-addicted-to-reverb), [Library of Congress NLS blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/nls-music-notes/2022/08/hidden-gems-of-the-nls-collection-the-music-of-khruangbin/))

Why that music fit: Thai pop of that era was itself a hybrid of surf and garage guitar, Western funk rhythm sections and local folk melody, often recorded cheaply and swimming in reverb. It was, in effect, a template for a band that wanted surf-adjacent guitar tone over funk drums without playing surf music. Speer names Thai singer Dao Bandon as a source of vocal melodies he mimics on guitar. ([POW](https://www.powmag.net/p/khruangbin-interview)) But the band pushes back hard on the "Thai funk" label: "To call us Thai funk is a great disservice to the people who made that music in the first place" (Speer), and "nobody in the band speaks Thai, and no one is of Thai descent" (Johnson). ([Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-con-todo-el-mundo-interview), [Line of Best Fit](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/features/interviews/khruangbin-in-conversation))

### Iranian pre-revolution pop

For the second album the frame widened to the Middle East, North Africa, Spain and the west of Ireland, and Speer says many of the scales are "Iranian and Persian in nature." ([Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/khruangbins-mark-speer-addicted-to-reverb)) The band found these records by crate-digging in every tour city, and were especially taken with 1970s Iranian pop, which blended Western funk, rock and soundtrack orchestration with "romantic, melancholy vocals." Lee: "I love the toughness of the bass and drums with the really romantic melancholy vocals in Iran's music." Their list of favorites runs from Googoosh (Speer calls her "the Michael Jackson of Iran") to Kourosh Yaghmaei, Sattar, Shahram Shabpareh and Vigen. ([The Vinyl Factory](https://thevinylfactory.com/features/khruangbin-favourite-iranian-records/), [Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-con-todo-el-mundo-interview)) That description, tough rhythm section under a sad, singing melody, is close to a description of a Khruangbin track with the voice swapped for guitar.

### Dub and the studio bands

Dub is the structural model. The band describes it as "a prayer... spacious, meditative, able to transport the listener to another realm." ([Line of Best Fit](https://thelineofbestfit.com/news/latest-news/khruangbin-to-release-dub-version-of-con-todo-el-mundo)) Scientist and King Tubby mixes are the reference for the bass ("the bass lines on dub records sound like me," Lee says), and in 2019 the band released *Hasta El Cielo*, a dub version of *Con Todo El Mundo*, with two bonus mixes by Scientist himself. ([POW](https://www.powmag.net/p/khruangbin-interview), [Line of Best Fit](https://thelineofbestfit.com/news/latest-news/khruangbin-to-release-dub-version-of-con-todo-el-mundo))

Speer's other declared models are house rhythm sections rather than guitar heroes: the Wrecking Crew, Muscle Shoals, the Funk Brothers, Sly and Robbie, Kassav', Iran's Roha Band. His approach to all of it: "steal from everybody," but "if we put it all together in a specific combination, then you won't recognize any of that information." ([Live for Live Music](https://liveforlivemusic.com/features/khruangbin-interview-2019/), [Bandcamp Daily, Mordechai](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-mordechai-interview))

## 4. The reverb-heavy guitar

Three things explain the guitar sound: the trio format, Speer's non-Western ear, and some deliberately odd gear choices.

**It fills a trio.** "There's only three of us, so I'm using ambient effects like reverb and delay to fill it out." Reverb is "a huge part of what I do." He does not rely on amp reverb because touring amps vary, so he carries an Electro-Harmonix Holy Grail on the spring setting. ([Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/khruangbins-mark-speer-addicted-to-reverb))

**It imitates instruments that have reverb built in.** Speer links his reverb explicitly to South and Central Asian instruments: a sitar "kind of has a built-in reverb," as does the Afghan rabab, which "creates this mystical kind of vibe." ([Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/khruangbins-mark-speer-addicted-to-reverb)) The Guitar World headline on the same subject is "I was always into tones that I didn't hear so much in Western music." ([Guitar World](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/khruangbins-mark-speer-i-was-always-into-tones-that-i-didnt-hear-so-much-in-western-music); only the headline was retrievable.)

**The gear is deliberately dead and dry.** He tours with a single 2001 Fender '70s-reissue Stratocaster, strung with D'Addario Chromes flatwounds that he changes about once a tour, because he hates the sound of new strings: "Once they're broken in, they're going to sound like that until they break." He has used the same worn green Tortex pick for years. A Cry Baby wah is on at all times, parked toe-down at its most trebly point, because he dislikes the click and tonal jump of switching it in. A wiring mistake put some pickup positions out of phase, giving a "Sears, Teisco-type vibe" that he kept. The amp is a Fender Deluxe Reverb (a beat-up '96 blackface reissue in the studio, '68 Custom silverface reissues on the road), with a Boss DS-1, a phaser, a compressor and a Strymon delay, though delays on the records were largely added in the mix. ([Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/khruangbins-mark-speer-addicted-to-reverb), [Mixdown](https://mixdownmag.com.au/features/gear-rundown-khruangbin/))

Flatwounds plus a fixed wah plus spring reverb on a Strat is, in effect, a recipe for the thin, dark, vocal, slightly "old cassette" tone of the Thai and Iranian records he loves, played through the one American amp every surf and soul guitarist used. One listener-critic describes the result as a "thin and sunburned" guitar with a "dry, locked-on wah" sound that is recognizable within seconds. ([John Barclay, Understanding Khruangbin](https://johnbarclay.substack.com/p/understanding-khruangbin))

**Lee's bass gear points the same direction.** Her SX bass still wears its original flatwounds (nine-plus years at last count), through a late-'60s Fender Bassman Ten chosen because it lacks the mid-scoop of modern bass amps, and an Acme Motown DI modeled on the Motown direct box. One always-on compressor, for consistency. ([Mixdown](https://mixdownmag.com.au/features/gear-rundown-khruangbin/), [Analogue Foundation](https://analoguefoundation.com/voice/khruangbin/), [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/features/artist-features/how-laura-lee-found-her-sound))

## 5. The barn: why the records sound spacious

The band formed and recorded almost everything through 2020 in a barn on Speer's family property in Burton, Texas (population about 300), roughly 90 minutes from Houston behind a house built in the late 1800s. It was chosen because it was free when Houston studios were not; it has dirt floors, corrugated tin walls, no climate control, hornets in the corners, and cows outside. Engineer Steve Christensen brings in a portable rig, sometimes with a mic placed out in the field. ([Live for Live Music](https://liveforlivemusic.com/features/khruangbin-interview-2019/), [Analogue Foundation](https://analoguefoundation.com/voice/khruangbin/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khruangbin))

Two consequences for the sound:

- **Space is left because the room is not silent.** Speer: studios are "very sterile, quiet spaces," which pressures players to fill every gap; in the barn, wind, birds, creaking wood and insects fill the gaps for you. Opening the doors dries the sound; closing them adds room. ([Austin Chronicle, ACL](https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/acl-fest-interview-khruangbin-11675045/), [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/khruangbins-mark-speer-addicted-to-reverb)) Lee calls the barn "the fourth member of the band... there's a resonance in the barn where the barn kind of sings," and "you can definitely hear the barn in the records." ([LOC blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/nls-music-notes/2022/08/hidden-gems-of-the-nls-collection-the-music-of-khruangbin/), [Austin Chronicle](https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/khruangbin-slows-it-down-houston-style-12102184))
- **Everything is cut live in one take.** Songs start as drum loops in Ableton, Lee writes bass over them, Speer adds guitar; then bass, drums and guitar are tracked together in the barn with no punch-ins, and only percussion and (sometimes) vocals are overdubbed later at SugarHill in Houston. Lee: "an important part of the recording should be that it is unfolding live." ([Analogue Foundation](https://analoguefoundation.com/voice/khruangbin/), [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/khruangbins-mark-speer-addicted-to-reverb)) Live, three-piece, one-take recording is another reason there is so much air in the mixes: there is literally nothing else on the tape.

*A La Sala* (2024) was the first record made elsewhere, in Christensen's brick-and-tin warehouse, which Speer says "sonically sounds very similar to the barn." ([Houston Press](https://www.houstonpress.com/?p=4292))

## 6. Why mostly instrumental

This is the question the band gets asked most, and the answer has four parts that reinforce each other.

**The guitar is the singer.** Speer: "In Khruangbin, the guitar is taking the place of the lead singer... I'm specifically trying to make it sing." He listens to singers more than guitarists, and the voice he is chasing is Lata Mangeshkar's: "I'm shaping my guitar tone to kind of fulfill the same timbre as a human voice," a voice that "just cuts through that mix." On one track he says he is "trying to do my best to emulate Lata Mangeshkar in my best possible way on a guitar." ([Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/khruangbins-mark-speer-addicted-to-reverb), [Houston Press](https://www.houstonpress.com/?p=4292), [Rolling Stone India](https://rollingstoneindia.com/?p=94563))

**The band's whole listening life was in languages they don't speak.** Thai, Farsi, Hindi, Jamaican patois. Speer's argument is that if music in a language you cannot understand still moves you, then "the melody, inflection, and the intent" are doing the work, and that is what he tries to reproduce on guitar. Of Carnatic music over a drone: "you can't just get by with having some really boring melody. It has to make you feel." ([Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-con-todo-el-mundo-interview), [Rolling Stone India](https://rollingstoneindia.com/?p=94563)) Speer also simply dislikes singing, which the Houston Press notes as part of the reason. ([Houston Press](https://www.houstonpress.com/?p=4292))

**The two instrumental traditions they came from.** The church band played gospel and R&B *instrumentals* every Sunday; dub is, by definition, the vocal stripped away and the rhythm section foregrounded. Both men spent years making music where the singer was absent or optional. Speer's gloss: "We are trying to say things musically like classical music can say so many things with no words." ([Houston Press](https://www.houstonpress.com/?p=4292))

**It is a deliberate choice to keep the music open.** Lee: the band writes instrumentals first and "if and only if the songs feel like they want another layer, do we add a vocal layer... I don't want to ever put the limit on ourselves to have to put vocals on anything." She values music that is "universal," and says instrumentals plus an unpronounceable non-English name let the band reach listeners outside any expected audience. ([emilyelhaj.com](https://www.emilyelhaj.com/blog/laura-lee-interview), [Austin Chronicle](https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/khruangbin-slows-it-down-houston-style-12102184)) Early on, label executives liked the instrumentals but wanted a vocal on top; Lee's read is that "people just want to sing on top of it." Johnson, as of 2024: "The music comes first." ([AP via Click2Houston](https://www.click2houston.com/entertainment/2024/12/30/in-grammy-spotlight-khruangbin-wants-to-let-the-music-speak-for-itself/)) When vocals do appear (more on *Mordechai*, 2020), they are mostly all three members singing in unison, treated as another texture: "We have no interest in doing vocal acrobatics." ([Bandcamp Daily, Mordechai](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/khruangbin-mordechai-interview))

## 7. Putting it together

Take a guitarist who started on bass, grew up on hip-hop and P-Funk, spent a decade playing instrumental gospel in a Houston megachurch, and dug for records in the city's Indian, West African and Mexican neighborhoods; who wants his guitar to sound like a Bollywood playback singer and his reverb to sound like a rabab. Pair him with the church's organist, who also produces beats for Houston rappers and whose idea of drumming is a sampled Clyde Stubblefield break slowed to DJ Screw speed. Give the third chair to a friend who learned bass from scratch by playing along to Roots Radics, Scientist, Motown and Serge Gainsbourg, and who was never taught to play busier. Feed all three a steady diet of 1960s Thai cassettes and pre-revolution Iranian pop, in which tough funk rhythm sections sit under sad, reverb-soaked melodies sung in languages none of them speak. Then put them in a tin barn with the doors open, cutting everything live in one take with nothing to fill the silence but wind and cows.

That is the sound: dub bass carrying the tune, breakbeat drums staying out of the way, a flatwound Strat singing where a vocalist would be, and enough reverb and empty space that the barn itself gets a credit. Johnson's description is the best one-liner: "It's what we sound like when we play together." ([Live for Live Music](https://liveforlivemusic.com/features/khruangbin-interview-2019/))
